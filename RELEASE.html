Readiness — the honest answer

Not completely factory-ready. Every mechanical part can be printed and every off-the-shelf part can be bought today, and the assembled robot runs Pollen's own trained policies in simulation — so a working prototype is buildable now. Three things block a factory release, all named in §1: the three custom circuit boards have no published design files (we have a footprint-accurate stand-in, not fabricable Gerbers), the mechanical drawing generator has two measured defects still being fixed, and while the sim meshes are now visually reconciled against the product photographs, a physical metrology (laser-scan) check has not been done. This dossier states what is done, what is not, and gives the complete build sequence for the prototype.

4Assembly — full sequence

Build order: trunk → hips → legs → neck → head → wiring → power-up. Every servo ID, bearing seat and joint is called out. Positions are world-frame (mm) unless a body frame is named. Derived from the measured joint tree (14 hinges).

Before you begin — three rules Servo IDs: set each XL330's ID and baud (1 Mbps, baud_rate=3) before fitting it — the bus is one daisy chain; a duplicate ID means disassembly. Bearings: all press-fit into printed seats; press the outer race only. Fasteners: M2 throughout; counts marked [community] are derived from hole-fitting on the sim meshes, not a Pollen BOM. FDM holes print 0.1–0.3 mm undersize — ream before assembly. Torque is unspecified: snug + a quarter-turn into plastic.

STEP 1Trunk body trunk_base

Parts: trunk-base ×1 · power-support ×1 · banana-pcb-locker ×1 · banana contact PCB · NP-F550 · XL330 ×2 · bearing 22×16×4 ×2
  1. Press the two 22×16×4 hip-yaw idler bearings into trunk_base at body-frame (6, ±17.5, −5).
  2. Set servo IDs 20 (left hip yaw) and 10 (right hip yaw). Bolt both XL330s hanging from trunk_base, horns down (−z), horn axes through world (6, ±17.5, 115). Each XL330 uses its own 4 × Ø2.0 + 8 × Ø1.6 pattern.
  3. Bolt power_support (battery cradle) to trunk_base — 8 × Ø1.6 M2 tap [community]; M2×6 into inserts.
  4. Clamp the banana contact PCB onto power_support's Ø3.9 pins with banana_pcb_locker — 2 × M2 through its Ø2.17 eyes.
  5. Leave the NP-F550 out until Step 7.

STEP 2Hips yaw2roll / bearing_roll, hip_l ×2

Per side: yaw2roll ×1 · bearing-roll ×1 · hip-bracket ×1 · XL330 ×2 · bearing 22×16×4 ×2
  1. Set IDs 21 (left hip roll) / 11 (right).
  2. Press one 22×16×4 into yaw2roll/bearing_roll at body (0, 16.5, 12.5) and one into each hip bracket at (21/25, 0, −18.5).
  3. Fix yaw2roll to the hip-yaw servo horn (spline joint); the 3 mm bearing_roll plate rides the idler bearing opposite the horn — 4 × M2 [community].
  4. Mount the hip-roll servo in yaw2roll (8 × M2 + 6 c'bore [community]); roll axis +x at world (22.5, ±17.5, 102.5).
  5. Fix hip-bracket to the hip-roll horn — 9 × M2 + 6 c'bore [community]. Hip-pitch axis +y at world (4, ±42.5, 102.5). Mirror for the right side.

STEP 3Legs upper_leg, leg (shin), ankle ×2

Per side: upper-leg ×1 · rigidity-plate ×1 · shin ×1 · ankle ×1 · foot ×1 · sole (TPU) ×1 · XL330 ×3 · bearing 22×16×4 ×2 · 15×10×3 ×1
  1. Set IDs 22 hip-pitch, 23 knee, 24 ankle (left); 12/13/14 (right).
  2. Fit hip-pitch + knee servos into the upper-leg housing (knee axis −y at (−31.8, ±38.5, 80.5)); press one 22×16×4 into the upper leg at body (22, 35.8, −4).
  3. Screw the 1 mm rigidity plate across the thigh — 4 × M2 [community].
  4. Hang the upper leg on the hip bracket: horn side to hip-pitch, idler side on the bracket bearing.
  5. Fit the ankle servo into the shin (the 8 mm stepped plate) — 6 × M2 + 6 c'bore [community]; join shin to the knee horn + idler bearing.
  6. Press the 15×10×3 bearing into the ankle bracket; fix the ankle to the ankle-servo horn (5 × M2 + 5 c'bore [community]).
  7. Screw the hard foot to the ankle (fixed, no joint); fit the TPU sole onto the foot, friction surface down.

STEP 4Neck neck, neck_pitch

Parts: neck-plate ×2 · neck-pitch-bracket ×1 · XL330 ×2 · bearing 22×16×4 ×1
  1. Set IDs 30 (neck pitch, bottom) and 31 (head pitch, top).
  2. The two 2 mm neck plates span the 50 mm neck with a servo at each end — 4 × M2 per plate [community]. Neck-pitch axis −y at world (26, 14.5, 152.4); head-pitch axis +y at (26, 14.5, 202.4).
  3. Bolt the ID-30 servo to the trunk (horn to the neck plates); bolt ID-31 at the top.
  4. Press one 22×16×4 into neck_pitch at body (0, 20.8, −14.5) — the head-yaw axis bearing; fix neck_pitch to the ID-31 horn — 12 × M2 + 4 c'bore [community].

STEP 5Head yaw_roll_motion, jaw_soft (head + electronics)

Parts: yaw-roll-motion ×1 · motor-support ×1 · face-part ×1 · eye-ring ×1 · M12 lens + holder · IMX219 · ToF · Radxa Zero 3W · Robot HAT · speaker · top/bottom head shells · jaw ×1 · jaw-soft + soft-mouth-top (TPU) · XL330 ×3 · bearing 22×16×4 ×2 · 15×10×3 ×1
  1. Set IDs 32 (head yaw), 33 (head roll), 34 (mouth).
  2. Press two 22×16×4 into yaw_roll_motion at body x = −16 and +18 (straddling the roll axis). Fix it onto the head-yaw axis: ID-32 horn below, neck_pitch's bearing above — 4 × M2 + 6 c'bore [community]. Head-yaw axis +z at world (26, 0, 221.1).
  3. Mount the ID-33 head-roll servo in yaw_roll_motion; roll axis −x at (8.1, 0, 235.6).
  4. Fix motor_support (head plate) to the ID-33 horn — it carries the ID-33/34 servos and the electronics.
  5. Press the 15×10×3 bearing for the mouth hinge; mount the jaw on the ID-34 horn + that bearing — 5 × M2 [community]. Mouth range −5° (closed) … +30° (open).
  6. Electronics: screw the M12 lens holder to the IMX219 board (3 × M2), thread the lens; the camera sits behind face_part at world (81.4, 0, 251.1), mounted upside down. ToF at (81.4, 22.4, 249.1), 22.4 mm left of the camera. Fit the eye-ring into face_part (10 × M2 + 4 tap [community]). Stack the Robot HAT on the Radxa 40-pin header.
  7. Fit the TPU lips: jaw_soft (8.4 mm) on the jaw, soft_mouth_top (3.3 mm) under the top shell.
  8. Close the shells: bottom head shell (4 × M2), top head shell (3 × M2), trunk shells L/R (7 and 6+1 × M2) [all community].

5Wiring

One Dynamixel bus, 1 Mbps, on /dev/ttyS2 via the HAT. Daisy-chain order and cut lengths (JST EH 3-pin, ROBOTIS X3P leads), measured off the placements. Physical map: 3-layout.svg; full table: CABLES.md.

Bus daisy chain — cut lengths (mm) HAT → id34 (35) → id33 (60) → id32 (50) → id31 (165 — crosses head-yaw + head-pitch; route at the servo flank to pay out the full ±170° sweep) → id30 (35) → imu200 board (120, crosses neck-pitch) → then it splits:
imu200 → id20 (40) → id21 (65) → id22 (125) → id23 (20) → id24 (95)  and  imu200 → id10 (40) → id11 (65) → id12 (125) → id13 (20) → id14 (95).
  1. ToF ↔ HAT: Stemma J5, JST-SH 4-pin, 35 mm (I²C 0x29).
  2. Speaker ↔ HAT: 70 mm.
  3. Battery → HAT: 340 mm — crosses all four neck/head joints; route bundled with the bus loom through the neck.
  4. Camera: CSI ribbon Radxa → camera, 15 mm floor, 22-pin 0.5 mm FFC.
  5. Microphone loom: CANNOT DETERMINE — part and placement unpublished.
  6. Voltage-drop check at 1 A/servo, 21 AWG: PASS (worst drop 0.55 V, HAT → ankle).

6Power-up & verification

The four steps below are the short form. The full procedure — 44 numbered tests from a never-powered robot to one that has walked, each with a pass/fail gate, the instrument it needs and the log it leaves behind — is TEST-PLAN.html. It runs electrical bring-up, servo identity and calibration, the sensors, the control loop, the two radios and the pairing PIN, the walk, and an endurance and thermal soak; it carries the 42-gate end-of-line checklist and the 16 questions a built unit answers that no document can.

  1. Fit the NP-F550. The runtime reads pack voltage through the servos' own Present Input Voltage(144) register (model.rs:99–113), which is consistent with servo VDD being battery-direct through the HAT — but that is an inference from Pollen's code, not a measurement. Whether production servos see the raw 6.6–8.4 V pack, above the XL330's published 3.7–6.0 V rating, is CANNOT DETERMINE; TEST-PLAN EB-04 is the meter that settles it.
  2. Scan the bus — expect IDs 10–14, 20–24, 30–34 and 200 (imu_to_dxl). The runtime asserts return_delay_time=0, baud_rate=3, pwm_slope=255, shutdown=52 at boot.
  3. Verify each joint range against the MJCF (e.g. left hip-yaw −25…+30°) before loading a policy.
  4. Load a policy: the shipped ONNX walk / sit-stand policies run at 50 Hz. Our meshes have been validated against Pollen's physics in simulation (§3.4).
