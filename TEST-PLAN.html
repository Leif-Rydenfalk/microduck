1Scope, verdicts and order

This plan proves one built unit. It is not a design review and it is not a DFM check — those are the manufacturing playbook and the mechanical drawings. It starts at a fully assembled robot that has never been powered and ends at a robot that has walked.

1.1 Three verdicts

Table 1. Every gate in this document answers with exactly one of these.
VerdictMeansWhat it does to the unit
PASSthe stated quantity was measured and it is inside the stated bandproceed to the next test
FAILthe stated quantity was measured and it is outside the stated bandstop. The unit does not proceed and does not ship
CANNOT DETERMINEthe quantity could not be measured, or no band exists to judge it againstnot a pass. Record the value that was seen, the reason it cannot be judged, and what would settle it
Never loosen a gate to make a unit pass. A gate that a good unit fails is a defect in this document and is fixed by regenerating it from spec/test-plan.json with the new basis written down — never by widening a band in the field. Every gate below carries a basis block naming whether the number came from a vendor datasheet, from Pollen's own source, from our simulation, or from a decision of this plan.

1.2 Order, and why it is this order

  1. Section 3 — electrical bring-up. A meter before a battery. The pack is not fitted until the rails have been surveyed on a current-limited bench supply.
  2. Section 4 — servo identity and calibration. Fifteen servos share one wire and every unassigned one answers to ID 1, so identity is assigned one servo at a time, off the bus.
  3. Section 5 — sensor checks. Independent of motion; run before anything moves so a sensor fault is never diagnosed through a gait.
  4. Section 6 — control-loop and daemon acceptance. The health verdict is the gate every later test rests on.
  5. Section 7 — radios, identity and provisioning. The radios, the name and the PIN - none of it moves a joint, and the walk test needs a bonded pad.
  6. Section 8 — walk acceptance. On the floor, with the robot off its stand for the first time.
  7. Section 9 — endurance, power and thermal. Last, because it costs an hour of bench time and it ends with a flat battery.
The stand is not optional. robotctl robot init powers the joints and ramps to the home pose over about two seconds — it moves every joint. Pollen's own cheat sheet says it plainly: have the robot on its stand. Every test before section 8 runs with both feet clear of the bench.

Determine whether the XL330 servos are fed the raw 2S pack (6.6-8.4 V, above their 6.0 V rating) or a regulated rail.

Loglogs/<serial>/EB-04-servo-vdd.csv

Procedure

    expect → record
  2. Measure pin 2 to pin 1 at the far end of the chain, at id24 and at id14.
