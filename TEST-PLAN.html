1Scope, verdicts and order

This plan proves one built unit. It is not a design review and it is not a DFM check — those are the manufacturing playbook (lane G, not yet in the repository) and the mechanical drawings (lane B, not yet in the repository). It starts at a fully assembled robot that has never been powered and ends at a robot that has walked.

1.1 Three verdicts

Table 1. Every gate in this document answers with exactly one of these.
VerdictMeansWhat it does to the unit
PASSthe stated quantity was measured and it is inside the stated bandproceed to the next test
FAILthe stated quantity was measured and it is outside the stated bandstop. The unit does not proceed and does not ship
CANNOT DETERMINEthe quantity could not be measured, or no band exists to judge it againstnot a pass. Record the value that was seen, the reason it cannot be judged, and what would settle it
Never loosen a gate to make a unit pass. A gate that a good unit fails is a defect in this document and is fixed by regenerating it from spec/test-plan.json with the new basis written down — never by widening a band in the field. Every gate below carries a basis block naming whether the number came from a vendor datasheet, from Pollen's own source, from our simulation, or from a decision of this plan.

1.2 Order, and why it is this order

  1. Section 3 — electrical. A meter before a battery. The pack is not fitted until the rails have been surveyed on a current-limited bench supply.
  2. Section 4 — servo identity. Fifteen servos share one wire and every unassigned one answers to ID 1, so identity is assigned one servo at a time, off the bus.
  3. Section 5 — sensors. Independent of motion; run before anything moves so a sensor fault is never diagnosed through a gait.
  4. Section 6 — control loop. The health verdict is the gate every later test rests on.
  5. Section 7 — walk. Last, on the floor, with the robot off its stand for the first time.
The stand is not optional. robotctl robot init powers the joints and ramps to the home pose over about two seconds — it moves every joint. Pollen's own cheat sheet says it plainly: have the robot on its stand. Every test before section 7 runs with both feet clear of the bench.

4Servo identity and calibration

Fifteen servos and one IMU board share one wire. Two EEPROM values must be set per servo at build time, because neither can be assigned over a shared bus; everything else robotd asserts at every boot. Mechanical zero is set by assembly, not by a register - robotd never writes a homing offset.

4.1 The ID map, and the home pose in counts

Encoder counts are computed here, not typed: count = 2048 + rad × 4096 / (2π) = 2048 + rad × 651.898647, from the vendor's 4096 pulse/rev resolution and bus.rs's 2048 = 0 rad. One count is 0.0879 degrees, so the ±1.0 degree calibration tolerance is ±11 counts.
