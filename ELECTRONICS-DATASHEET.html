§1Architecture at a glance

A Radxa Zero 3W in the head is the only compute. One UART becomes a half-duplex Dynamixel bus carrying all fifteen XL330 servos plus the IMU-to-Dynamixel bridge as a sixteenth device. Everything else — audio codec, 8×8 ToF, camera, NFC, USB-C — hangs off the Radxa's I²C3 / I²S3 / CSI / USB through the custom RPI Robot HAT. Power is a removable 2S camera battery, read only through the servos' own voltage register.

Compute

Radxa Zero 3W — Rockchip RK3566 quad Cortex-A55, 1 GB RAM, 32 GB eMMC, Wi-Fi 6 / BT 5.4, 65 × 30 mm. Runs Armbian; the RK3566's 0.8-TOPS NPU runs a duck detector at 2 Hz (thermal-limited).

Actuation

15 × ROBOTIS Dynamixel XL330-M288-T, one 1 Mbps Protocol-2 TTL bus on /dev/ttyS2 (RK3566 UART2_M0). IDs 10–14 / 20–24 / 30–34; mouth = ID 34, excluded from the 14-DoF policy.

Sensing

Control IMU LSM6DSV16X on the imu_to_dxl board (bus ID 200); 8×8 ToF VL53L5CX/L8CX; IMX219 camera + M12 lens (mounted inverted); dormant BMI088; mics; two NFC antennas.

Power

Sony-type NP-F550 2S Li-ion, 2600 mAh, 18.72 Wh, removable. No fuel gauge — state of charge is inferred from the voltage the servos report. USB-C 5 V charges the board; the FUSB302 PD node is disabled.

§3The servo bus

One wire does everything. Fifteen servos and the IMU bridge share a single 3.3 V-logic (5 V-tolerant) TTL half-duplex UART at 1 Mbit/s under DYNAMIXEL Protocol 2.0. There is no second bus and no second port (robotd-design.md).

§5Control-table registers & the tick

Written once at boot (robotd, per servo)

return_delay_time = 0 (vendor default 250 → 0) baud_rate = 3 (1 Mbps) pwm_slope = 255 shutdown = 52 (overload | overheat | volt fault; vendor 53 with input-volt bit cleared)

Rationale (model.rs): the default 250 is 500 µs turnaround × 16 devices = 8 ms/tick, 40 % of a 20 ms budget — so it is zeroed.

Read every tick / every second

50 Hz loop (20 ms): sync_read regs 124–136 IMU block + 15 servos (present pos / vel / load) sync_write goal positions 1 Hz slow: read regs 144–146 present_input_voltage (0.1 V/LSB), temperature Gains: P = 200, I = D = 0 standing_gain_ratio 0.8 · gain_limp 50 on predicted fall
The imu_to_dxl board answers ID 200 in the same sync_read: register block at address 124 carries 6 bytes gyro (int16, 17.5 mdps/LSB) + 6 bytes SFLP game-rotation quaternion (x/y/z as fp16, w reconstructed). One IMU, one code path (imu.rs).

The one anomaly worth flagging: the XL330 datasheet input band is 3.7–6.0 V, but the pack runs 6.6–8.2 V and the firmware’s Max-Voltage-Limit tops at 7.0 V. Either production servos are stock parts run over-spec, or there is a regulated rail we cannot see. A meter on a servo VDD pin settles it. Bus voltage-drop is nonetheless in spec: 21 AWG at 6.6 V still delivers 6.05 V to the far ankle (wiring/drop.json).

§7The three custom boards

Pollen publishes no schematic, Gerber or BOM for any of these; the facts below are read from the firmware and device trees. Our own reconstruction of the HAT as a ce-pcb board is in progress under electronics/.

RPI Robot HAT (P1) — 65 × 30 mm

On the Radxa's 40-pin header. Carries the TLV320AIC3104 codec (0x18), the dormant BMI088, the ToF via a Stemma J5 connector (0x29), the half-duplex bus transceiver, the battery-to-board power path and a 10k I²C pull-up pair (R12/R13). Disables the Radxa's FUSB302 PD node.

imu_to_dxl v2 (P2)

Presents an LSM6DSV16X as a Dynamixel Protocol-2 slave at ID 200, register block 124. Rides the servo bus. MCU, transceiver and firmware are unpublished.

Banana battery-contact PCB (P3)

Clamped in the trunk by the printed banana_pcb_locker; carries the NP-F550 contacts to the HAT. Shape and contact layout unpublished.
