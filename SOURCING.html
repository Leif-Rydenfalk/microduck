1 How to read this

Five rules decide every number on this page. They are stored beside the data in spec/sourcing.json, not in this prose, so the generator and the reader cannot drift apart.

A price is a reading

unit price at the vendor's own published tier. tiers[] is [order_quantity_of_the_part, unit_price]. A vendor that publishes no tier table carries tiers [[1, price]] and no_tiers=true; its @1 price is then used at every quantity as a CEILING, never as a forecast.

Quantity model

For N robots the order quantity of a line is qty_per_robot x N. The unit price used is the vendor's largest tier whose quantity is <= that order quantity. If the vendor's MOQ exceeds the order quantity the MOQ tier price is used and moq_exceeds_need is set.

No currency is converted

Left exactly as the vendor states it. NO FX rate was fetched, so no conversion is performed anywhere. Only USD offers enter the USD roll-up; EUR / GBP / NZD / AUD / INR lines are reported beside it, unconverted.

An unknown is never zero

The roll-up publishes TWO subtotals: the readable subtotal (lines with a read USD price) and the CANNOT DETERMINE list (lines with none). An unknown is NEVER summed as zero.

The three verdicts

PASS = two or more distributors with a price read on a page, and a unit price determinable at 1 / 100 / 1000 robots. CANNOT DETERMINE = fewer than two read prices, or none. FAIL = the line as specified cannot be bought from anyone (the part does not exist in the maker's own catalogue).

Nothing was sent

No supplier was contacted, no order was placed and no money was spent producing this document. RFQ.html is written and ready; sending it is a human decision.

Two things this table does on purpose. A line whose need is smaller than the vendor's minimum — 0.2183 kg of a 1 kg filament spool, 60 of a 100-piece insert pack, 11 of a 1 000-piece bearing lot — is prorated: the per-robot figure is the unit price times the per-robot quantity, so the remainder of the pack is charged to the next robot rather than to this one. At a build of exactly one robot you pay the whole pack, and the MOQ > need flag is where that is said. And the cheapest read vendor is chosen per line and per quantity independently, so a line can change supplier between the @1 and the @100 column — B9 moves from DigiKey to Pololu, B6 from Seeed's @1 to Seeed's 10+ tier. Nothing is blended.
