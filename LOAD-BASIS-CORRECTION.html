Microduck · structural evidence · correction notice

The landing load was wrong by a factor of 2.77

Every “the ankle fails in every material” result this project produced came from a load case labelled 3× bodyweight that actually applied 8.30× bodyweight. This document states the error, the arithmetic, the re-solved numbers, and what is and is not still a problem.

1The error

sim/stress_matrix.py declared its landing case as “3× bodyweight on one leg — step-off / drop” and applied 60 N. The arithmetic does not support that label:

mass 737 g
weight 0.737 × 9.81 = 7.230 N
two-legged stance 7.230 / 2 = 3.615 N per leg
slow-walk peak 1.3 × 7.230 = 9.399 N (single stance)
3× bodyweight 3 × 7.230 = 21.690 N ← what the label claimed
applied 60.000 N = 8.30× bodyweight ← what was solved
Consequence. The ankle result reported all night — SF 0.674, “FAILS in every material, no filament fixes it” — was produced by a load 2.77× larger than the one it was named for. That claim was broadcast to a peer session twice and written into a commit message and a handover register. It was wrong.

2Re-solved, not re-scaled

Linear elasticity predicts that the safety factor scales inversely with load, so the corrected figures were predictable. A prediction is not a measurement, so every corrected case was solved and the two are printed side by side. The axial cases agree to −0.00 %, which validates the scaling law on this geometry; the lateral rows disagree by design, because their load direction differs from the axial baseline the prediction was scaled from and scaling across directions is meaningless.

3What is still a problem, and what is not

The ankle does not yield at a real 3× bodyweight landing. Corrected SF 1.864 at 21.690 N. It misses the 2.0 design target by 6.8 %, which is a margin shortfall — a modest reinforcement or an explicit decision to accept 1.86, not the crisis the 0.674 figure implied. The material sweep that showed PLA/PETG/ABS/NYLON/ASA all below 1.0 was measuring the 60 N case and says nothing about this one.
The shin and hip bracket were never close to the limit. Shin SF 6.925 at the corrected landing; hip bracket passes standing and lateral.
A genuine drop or impact load remains CANNOT DETERMINE. Peak force in a drop depends on height and contact compliance, and this is a static linear solver with no impact model. 3× bodyweight is the walking design figure, not a drop. If the product must survive being knocked off a desk, that is a different question and this document does not answer it.
microduck-upper-leg-left is still unverified, and now provably so. Every surface mesher, both volume meshers and the tessellation route refuse its geometry at every overlap tolerance. FreeCAD reports the solid valid, closed and free of self-intersections; gmsh finds overlapping facets. The thigh sits in the same load path as the ankle and carries no structural verdict at all. The same triangulation feeds the slicer, so its printed toolpaths are suspect too.

4Method and limits

  • Solver. gmsh quadratic tetrahedra (C3D10) + CalculiX, linear elastic static, isotropic, PLA. One part at a time, no contact.
  • Mesh convergence is still not established for the ankle. It meshes only at the automatic characteristic length; 2.0 / 1.5 / 1.0 mm all return CANNOT DETERMINE. Both SF 0.674 and SF 1.864 rest on a single mesh. The ratio between them is trustworthy — same mesh, same geometry, load scaled — but the absolute value is not convergence-verified.
  • FDM parts are anisotropic. These figures use along-layer properties, so for a printed part they are upper bounds unless the load lies in the print plane.
  • No fatigue, creep or plasticity. A part that passes a single static case can still fail after ten thousand steps.
  • What this correction does not touch: the standing and lateral cases were always solved at the loads they claimed, so their verdicts stand unchanged.
